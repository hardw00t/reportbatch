# Commercial hypothesis — unvalidated, no checkout

## Observed workflow commissions

Checked 2026-10-18. These public listings show people commissioning repeatable PDF workflows. They do **not** establish demand, customers, revenue or sales for ReportBatch. We did not contact these buyers, download their documents, bid, or copy their work.

- [Automate PDF Report Splitting](https://www.freelancer.com.au/projects/automation/automate-pdf-report-splitting): listed completed, A$30–250 budget; describes an initial 139-page report batch, with roughly 3,900 pages to follow. Names should use report-header date, author and type. This supports investigating repeatable fixed-layout extraction and page accounting. ReportBatch's current 500-page cap does not handle that larger batch in one run.
- [PDF Invoice Split to Drive](https://www.freelancer.com/projects/python/pdf-invoice-split-drive): listed completed, US$30–250 budget; asks for grouped invoices named from invoice/customer data and uploaded to Drive. The live page checked here displays an awarded proposal of **US$100**; US$150 appears on other proposals. Earlier research supplied a US$150 awarded figure, which this check does not confirm. ReportBatch deliberately excludes Drive integration and automatic value-change grouping.

Listed budgets and proposal amounts are not independently verified final payments. Two examples cannot establish market size, customer acquisition cost or willingness to pay for this product.

## Existing alternatives

This is an established category; local processing, region naming and previews are not unique functionality.

| Alternative | Vendor-documented overlap |
| --- | --- |
| [AutoSplit](https://evermap.com/AutoSplit-Split.asp), [location-based naming](https://evermap.com/Tutorial_ASP_NamingPDFUsingTextFromLocation.asp) | Acrobat-integrated splitting, text-based naming and region-based extraction; Pro adds automation. |
| [PDFsam Visual](https://pdfsam.org/split-by-content/) | Local splitting by content and naming using the text that triggers a split. |
| [ScanRoute Local](https://automatalabs.ca/rename-pdf-based-on-content/) | Local split-and-name workflow with a drawn naming zone and review of proposed names. |

ReportBatch's proposed positioning is a focused workflow: versioned fixed-layout presets, full-batch blockers instead of silent guesses, explicit missing/ambiguous values, and a deterministic manifest mapping every source page to its output. This is a packaging and service hypothesis, not a claim competitors lack these capabilities. Vendor descriptions are not hands-on competitive tests.

## Proposed service to validate: US$149 per configured layout

The code is MIT-licensed. A possible **one-time US$149 configured-workflow service** would include one supported fixed-layout preset (up to eight required fields), a naming template, one explicit grouping rule, a local installation walkthrough and an acceptance checklist on operator-supplied synthetic/redacted examples. Proposed scope: one revision, up to 500 pages/30 MiB per batch within the published limits. No recurring fee, OCR, integrations, legal/accounting advice, unlimited support or guaranteed accuracy is implied.

This is not an active offer and no sale or payment is enabled. The price is a testable proposal, not a validated price point or revenue forecast. Before offering it, validate whether target operators can configure and review their workflow, whether this service saves enough repeat work, and whether they would pay the proposed amount. Any interviews, customer document handling, outreach, payment setup or hosting require separate authorization; none occurred in this work.

## Practical next acceptance questions

Can an operator get a correct first export from the synthetic sample quickly? Can a second batch reuse a preset without storing source content? Are exceptions visible and recoverable? Does the page trail make review easier? Can the proposed service be delivered within its fixed scope? Product QA can answer the first four on fixtures; customer willingness to pay remains untested.
